# Reflection

This assignment helped me understand how important prompt design is in building AI systems. Initially, I assumed that simply giving a basic instruction to the model would be enough to generate good responses. However, I quickly realized that without detailed prompts, the responses were generic and lacked personality.

The biggest learning for me was the GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out) principle. When my prompts were vague, the output was also vague. Once I added structured prompts with clear persona definitions, few-shot examples, and constraints, the responses improved significantly. The chatbot started sounding more like real people instead of a generic AI.

Another important insight was the role of few-shot examples. They helped guide the tone, structure, and depth of the responses. For example, adding realistic examples for Anshuman Singh made the model respond in a more data-driven and confident way.

I also learned that constraints are equally important. Without constraints, the model could easily generate incorrect or unrealistic information. Adding rules like “never make up statistics” ensured consistency and reliability.

If I had more time, I would improve the system by adding memory so that conversations feel more continuous. I would also refine the prompts further using more real-world references and edge cases.

Overall, this assignment showed me that prompt engineering is not just about writing instructions, but about designing behavior and shaping how the AI thinks and responds.
